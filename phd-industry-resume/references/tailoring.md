# Tailoring to a role

Same facts, different emphasis. Tailoring never means inventing experience; it means choosing
which true things to put first and which words to use.

## Reading a job ad

Work through the ad with the user before rewriting anything:

1. **What does the person in this job actually do all day?** Ignore the company blurb. Look at the
   responsibilities list and translate it into plain verbs (interview users, write briefs, build
   models, manage stakeholders, run workshops).
2. **What are the 3–5 things they care most about?** Clues: what's listed first, what's repeated,
   what's under "essential" rather than "desirable", and what the title emphasises.
3. **What language do they use?** If they say "stakeholders", "customers", "citizens" or "users",
   mirror it. Applicant tracking systems and skimming recruiters both match on words. Use their
   terms where they're accurate for the user's experience; don't keyword-stuff.
4. **Are there selection criteria?** In Australian government (APS and state), universities and
   many NFPs, these are scored and must be addressed, usually in a separate statement. See
   `australia.md`.
5. **What's the level?** Graduate, mid-level, senior? It changes which experiences to lead with and
   how much leadership to claim.

Then build a short map for the user:

| What they want | Best evidence you have | Gap? |
|---|---|---|
| e.g. Run mixed-methods studies | PhD fieldwork: 40 interviews + survey of 300 | none |
| e.g. Influence product decisions | Findings adopted by industry partner | thin, ask for more |

Be honest about gaps. Then ask whether they have something that fills them (side projects,
volunteering, teaching, a placement, consulting). If they genuinely don't, say so, and suggest
they address it in the cover letter or interview rather than stretching the resume.

## What to change when tailoring

- **Summary:** rewrite it for this role every time. It should read as if they've been heading
  towards this job.
- **Key skills line:** reorder and swap so the ad's top priorities come first.
- **Bullets:** reframe the 3–6 most relevant ones (see "one bullet, three angles" in `bullets.md`).
- **Order:** move the most relevant role or project up, even within a section.
- **Cut:** anything that doesn't support this application. Less is more.
- **Filename:** `Firstname_Lastname_Resume_Organisation.pdf`.

## Role patterns

Use these as starting points. Always check against the actual ad.

### UX research, design research, service design

- **They care about:** talking to users, turning findings into product or design decisions, mixed
  methods, working with designers and product managers, speed.
- **Lead with:** studies run, participants recruited, methods used, and what changed in a product,
  service or guideline as a result.
- **Metrics that matter:** number of participants or sessions, studies delivered, decisions
  influenced, adoption of findings, time to insight.
- **Cut or shrink:** theory, publication lists, literature reviews.
- **Portfolio:** expected. Two or three short case studies beat a long list.

### Government and policy

- **They care about:** evidence for decisions, working with stakeholders and communities, writing
  clearly for decision-makers, understanding how government works, integrity.
- **Lead with:** partnerships with government or community bodies, briefs or submissions written,
  evidence that informed a policy, strategy or procurement decision, community engagement scale.
- **Metrics that matter:** stakeholders engaged, residents or communities reached, policies or
  strategies informed, submissions made, inquiries or hearings.
- **Watch for:** selection criteria (address every one), APS capability framework language
  (e.g. "supports strategic direction", "achieves results"), security clearance and citizenship
  requirements.
- **Publications:** list selectively only for research-heavy policy roles.

### Consulting (strategy, design or research consultancies)

- **They care about:** delivering for clients to deadline, framing problems, synthesising fast,
  presenting to senior people, commercial awareness.
- **Lead with:** projects delivered end to end, value of work or funding, external partners or
  "clients" (industry partners, funders, government bodies count), presentations to senior
  audiences.
- **Metrics that matter:** number and value of projects, budgets, timelines met, partners managed.
- **Tip:** treat each major PhD project or study as a "client engagement" with a sponsor, a
  deliverable and an outcome.

### Data science and analytics

- **They care about:** tools (Python, R, SQL, cloud platforms), statistics, modelling, working with
  messy real data, communicating results to non-technical people, impact on decisions.
- **Lead with:** concrete technical work with scale (dataset sizes, models built, pipelines
  automated) and what the analysis changed.
- **Metrics that matter:** data volumes, accuracy or performance improvements, time saved,
  decisions or products affected.
- **Skills line:** be specific (pandas, scikit-learn, PostgreSQL, AWS), not "data analysis".
- **Portfolio:** a GitHub profile with one or two clean, documented projects helps a lot.

### Industry R&D and research scientist

- **They care about:** deep expertise, rigour, novelty, ability to turn research into products,
  publication or patent record (sometimes).
- **Lead with:** technical depth and research outcomes, but still framed as delivery.
- **Publications:** list a selected 3–5, most relevant first.
- **Still cut:** teaching duties, committees, conference attendance.

### Product management and strategy

- **They care about:** prioritisation, working across teams, customer understanding, outcomes and
  metrics, communication with leadership.
- **Lead with:** anything they owned end to end, decisions they made with evidence, coordinating
  people with different goals (supervisors, partners, funders).
- **Metrics that matter:** outcomes delivered, adoption, people coordinated, budgets.

### Not-for-profit, program and engagement roles

- **They care about:** mission fit, community engagement, program delivery, grant writing,
  reporting to funders.
- **Lead with:** outreach, events, community partnerships, funding secured, people reached.
- **Metrics that matter:** participants or audiences, funding won, programs delivered, partners.

## When the user has no target yet

Don't tailor into a vacuum. Suggest they pick one or two directions and build a base resume that
leans towards them. Offer to sketch two versions of their summary (e.g. one for UX research, one
for policy) so they can see which feels more like them.
