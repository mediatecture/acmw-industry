# Australian conventions (and differences elsewhere)

This skill defaults to the Australian job market. Use these conventions unless the user says
they're applying elsewhere.

## Australia

- **Call it a resume.** "CV" is also understood, but an Australian industry employer expects a
  resume of 1–3 pages, not an academic CV.
- **Spelling:** Australian English (organisation, analyse, programme/program, centre, behaviour).
- **No photo, date of birth, marital status, nationality or gender.** These invite bias and most
  employers would rather not see them. Project images can work for design or creative roles.
- **Contact line:** name, professional email, mobile number in Australian format
  (04xx xxx xxx), city or region (no street address needed), LinkedIn or portfolio link.
- **Work rights:** if the user is on a visa, a short line like "Full working rights in Australia"
  can help, but only if it's accurate. Never guess their status; ask if it's relevant.
- **Referees:** "Referees available on request" is unnecessary; leave it off. Have 2–3 ready.
- **File format:** PDF, with a sensible filename.
- **Salary:** if the ad asks for expectations, look up the range first (SEEK, Hays or Robert
  Walters salary guides, APS enterprise agreements for government roles). Don't put salary on the
  resume itself.

### Government roles (APS, state and local)

- **Selection criteria** or a "pitch" (often a 1–2 page statement or a set of 250–500 word
  responses) are scored separately from the resume. Address every criterion with a specific
  example, ideally in STAR form. Missing one can rule the application out.
- **APS levels** (APS 1–6, EL1, EL2, SES): PhD graduates often enter at APS 5–6 or EL1 depending on
  experience. Graduate programs exist but are often aimed at less experienced candidates.
- The APS Commission publishes **work level standards** and a **leadership capability framework**
  describing what each level is expected to do. Mirroring that language (where accurate) helps.
  The framework has been revised over time, so point users to the current version on the APSC
  website rather than quoting it from memory.
- **Citizenship and security clearance** requirements are common. Flag them if the ad mentions
  them.
- State governments (e.g. VPS in Victoria, NSW Public Service) have similar but different systems;
  check the ad.

### Universities (professional, non-academic roles)

- Often also use selection criteria. Treat them like government roles.

### Contracting and consulting

- An **ABN** (Australian Business Number) lets you invoice as a sole trader for contract or
  consulting work. Worth getting soon after graduation if contracting is a possibility; it's free
  and quick. Mention it only if relevant to the application.

## Other markets (brief)

- **United Kingdom:** "CV" is the normal word even in industry, but expect 2 pages. No photo. A
  personal statement at the top is common.
- **United States:** "Resume", 1 page for early career, 2 at most. No photo, no age. Very
  metrics-driven. Federal government roles use their own longer format.
- **Canada:** like the US, usually 2 pages.
- **Continental Europe:** a photo and date of birth are still common in some countries (e.g.
  Germany, Belgium, France), and the Europass format exists, though many international and tech
  employers prefer the shorter, photo-free style. Check what the employer expects.
- **New Zealand:** close to Australian conventions.

If the user is applying in another country, adapt length and personal-detail conventions, but the
core principles (front-loaded, outcomes over tasks, numbers, tailoring) hold everywhere.
