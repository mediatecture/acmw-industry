# Rewriting bullets

## The method

### STAR, compressed

Every strong bullet carries some version of:

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| **S**ituation | the problem | why this mattered |
| **T**ask | the goal | what you set out to do |
| **A**ction | what you did | the specific thing *you* did |
| **R**esult | what changed | the outcome, ideally with a number |

On a resume, Situation and Task are often implied or squeezed into a few words. **Action and
Result must always be there.** A bullet with no result is a job description, not an achievement.

### The three questions

When a bullet is weak, ask these in order. Each one usually produces a better rewrite:

1. **What did I actually do?** Strip away passive phrasing and team credit. What did *you* build,
   run, design, recruit, write or decide?
2. **So what? Who cared?** What came of it? Who used it, funded it, covered it, adopted it?
3. **For this role, what matters?** The same work can be framed for a UX job, a policy job or a
   data job. Pick the angle the reader is hiring for (see `tailoring.md`).

### Action verbs

Lead with one. Prefer verbs that show ownership and outcome:

- **Leading:** led, directed, managed, coordinated, chaired, supervised, mentored
- **Making:** built, designed, developed, prototyped, wrote, created, launched, shipped
- **Winning:** secured, won, raised, negotiated, attracted
- **Improving:** redesigned, streamlined, reduced, increased, lifted, rebuilt, automated
- **Finding out:** ran, recruited, interviewed, analysed, tested, evaluated, surveyed, modelled
- **Sharing:** taught, presented, published, briefed, trained, facilitated

Avoid: responsible for, involved in, helped with, assisted, participated in, exposure to,
worked on, was part of.

### Filler to cut

| Academic phrasing | Industry phrasing |
|---|---|
| was involved in | helped build / ran / led (whichever is true) |
| assisted with data collection | collected data from [n] … |
| responsible for | (just say what you did) |
| it could be argued that | delete it |
| a novel approach to | a new way to |
| contributed to the literature on | (say who used the findings) |
| gained experience in | (say what you delivered with it) |
| developed an understanding of | delete it; nobody hires for understanding |

## Translating PhD work into industry skills

Academics often only see themselves as **experts**. Use this table to show them the other roles
they've already done. Ask which apply, then find the evidence.

| Industry role | PhD evidence to look for |
|---|---|
| **Expert** | Deep domain knowledge, methods, technical tools |
| **Storyteller** | Conference talks, public lectures, media interviews, writing for non-specialists, exhibitions, outreach |
| **Investigator** | Research design, interviews, fieldwork, experiments, data analysis, synthesis |
| **Educator** | Tutoring, lecturing, curriculum design, supervising students, running workshops |
| **Project lead** | Running a multi-year project to deadline, coordinating fieldwork, managing a budget, ethics approvals, organising events |
| **Fundraiser** | Grants, scholarships, travel funding, industry partnerships, in-kind support |
| **Collaborator** | Co-authors across disciplines, industry or government partners, community groups, international visits |

## Worked examples

These show the level of specificity to aim for. Some are illustrative and some come from real
resumes with the numbers changed. Never borrow these numbers for a user.

### Teaching

> **Before:** Sessional tutor for Introduction to Robotics (2024–2026).
>
> **After:** Taught weekly labs of 25 first-year students across 4 semesters; redesigned C++
> exercises around hands-on challenges, lifting student satisfaction from 3.8 to 4.5.

What changed: a role title became an action (taught), scale (25 students, 4 semesters), an
initiative (redesigned exercises) and a measured result (3.8 → 4.5).

### Fieldwork

> **Before:** Conducted interviews for PhD research.
>
> **After:** Recruited and interviewed 40 farmers across regional Victoria, building a dataset now
> used by two other research projects.

What changed: recruitment is named as work (it's hard, and industry researchers do it), the scale
is specific, and the result shows someone else valued the work.

### Operations (post-PhD industry role)

> **Before:** Responsible for customer interviews and research operations.
>
> **After:** Rebuilt research operations: 4x customer contact (~25 to ~100 interviews a quarter)
> and 20% lower operating costs, by consolidating tools and livestreaming all interviews.

What changed: "responsible for" became "rebuilt", with two business metrics and the how in brief.

### Public engagement

> **Before:** Created an AI art installation exhibited at several venues, which attracted media
> attention.
>
> **After:** Reached an estimated 250m people through media coverage (equivalent to ~$250k in
> advertising). Secured $180k to build a touring museum exhibit, and gave talks at major
> international policy forums.

What changed: "attracted media attention" became reach with a dollar equivalent, plus funding won
and who invited them.

### One bullet, three angles

The same PhD work can be rewritten several ways. This shows the three questions in action.

> **Before:** Ran 4 urban field studies, with findings published in peer-reviewed venues.

**What did I actually do?**
> Designed and built 4 interactive displays and evaluated them with local residents, with support
> from councils and an industry partner.

**So what? Who cared?**
> Published findings at international venues (20–25% acceptance), with coverage on national TV,
> radio and newspapers.

**For a UX research role:**
> Ran 4 field studies with 32 residents to test how people use public technology; findings shaped
> design guidelines adopted by industry partners.

**For a government or policy role:**
> Partnered with 2 city councils to test digital tools for community participation, reaching ~200
> residents and informing [council]'s digital out-of-home procurement strategy.

Point out to users: none of these is "the right one". Each is true, and each is right for a
different reader. That's why one resume can't serve every application.

## Checklist for a finished bullet

- Starts with an action verb
- Says what *they* did, not what the project or team did
- Has a number, or a concrete outcome or named partner if no number exists
- Shows a result or who cared
- Fits on one to two lines
- Contains no word from the "give it away" list in `rules.md`
- Would make sense to someone outside their field
- They could talk about it for five minutes in an interview
